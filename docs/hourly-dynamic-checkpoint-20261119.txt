Hourly risk predictions and uncapped dynamic checkpoint scheduling — 2026-11-19

Authority and paper evidence
The researcher explicitly requested removal of the default600-second cap and
an hourly recovery point when lambda=0 or the policy retains no Spot workers.
Cost-Efficient Training and Checkpointing for Large Models on Preemptible Cloud
VMs (Desai et al.), provided original Cost_effective.pdf, section4.1: PT=1hour
prediction intervals; section4.2: recompute checkpoint interval on prediction
changes. Faster source collection/polling does not represent a new forecast.
A1-hour prediction interval is not a1-hour checkpoint maximum. The1-hour
zero-hazard recovery-point fallback is our operational rule, not a paper optimum.

Implementation
internal/policy/interval_bounds.go: absent maxIntervalSeconds now means only the
member scheduler int32 representation limit, not600seconds. Explicit operator
bounds remain supported for fixed controls and legacy compatibility. Final B/C
must share the same uncapped configuration before freezing. Historical receipts

The seconds-domain CRIU objective a/x+max(0,b/x-d)+c*x is unchanged. Positive
hazard with measured costs uses its existing analytic integer optimum. Missing
measurements retain the existing explicitly labelled bootstrap risk bands.
Zero hazard or zero policy Spot count uses3600seconds, even without invented
cost measurements; intervalSource=hourly-risk-refresh-fallback and
intervalCostEvaluated=false. Unknown/stale risk remains gated; it is not zero.
CheckpointIntervalDecision supplies the same provenance to both controllers.
The coordinator now reads the same UID-bound, Verified OD-residency receipts
as the allocation controller; a retained all-OD group must not be scheduled as
if proposed future Spot workers were already active.
Measured-cost freshness remains one selected interval plus600seconds grace:
when no explicit max is configured, use status.checkpoint.checkpointIntervalSeconds
rather than an absent max (which previously expired costs in600seconds).
Original observedAt is retained; future and expired measurements are rejected.
No int32-limit-long freshness is assumed merely because the cap was omitted.

Safety and interpretation
Periodic scheduling remains a stable FluidCR parent. A changed interval updates
its schedule; no duplicate parent or fabricated completion is created. Hourly
fallback is an elapsed periodic interval, not a guarantee of exact wall-clock
alignment with a source publication. Native forecast updates remain3600seconds.
Emergency checkpoint, in-flight exclusion, replacement quiescence, archive
hash/provenance, source fencing and Verified restore gates remain in force.
Full-group restore and the resource replacement algorithm are not redesigned.
The seconds-domain CRIU adaptation is not evidence that asynchronous GPU-to-DRAM
buffer assumptions of the paper hold in our runtime.
Longer periodic intervals can increase rollback after an unpredicted failure;
removing600seconds does not guarantee better completion time or lower cost.
The current group selector has no checkpoint age cutoff, but still requires a
complete world-size round with ownership, same-round and durable archive checks.
Do not silently reinterpret old capped pilot timings as uncapped measurements.
